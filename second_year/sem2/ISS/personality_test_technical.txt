We have 6 trait axes:

A (Abstraction) - do they enjoy working with mathematical concepts, models, and theory?
V (Visibility) — does the person care about what things look like? High V → frontend, UX. Low V → backend, data.
I (Interaction) — do they prefer working with people or machines/systems? High I → PM, support. Low I → DevOps, data.
D (Depth) — do they love digging into how things work under the hood? High D → backend, DevOps, data.
C (Creativity) — do they prefer open-ended problems or well-defined tasks? High C → frontend, UX, PM.
P (Pace) — do they like juggling many things at once or taking one problem deep? Used as a tiebreaker mostly.

Each individual question is answered on a 1–5 scale (e.g. "Strongly disagree → Strongly agree"), and then we average those answers to get the trait score.
Each trait's final score (1–5) is the average of the answers to its 4 questions.

Score per role:

Frontend Developer: V*2 + C*2 + P
Backend Developer: D*2 + (5−V)*2 + P
UI/UX Designer: V*3 + C*2 + I
DevOps Engineer: D*2 + P*2 + (5−I)
Project Manager: I*3 + C + (5−D)
Data Analyst: D*2 + A*2 + (5−I)
Cybersecurity Specialist: D*3 + (6−I) + (6−P)
AI/ML Engineer: D*3 + C + A*2

The personality test result shall be stored in the user profile and prominently displayed on the profile page formatted as "PERSONALITY TEST RESULT: [Role]" (e.g., "PERSONALITY TEST RESULT: BACKEND DEVELOPER").
The top 3 roles will be displayed there.
